Gender-inclusive Hebrew

Write nonbinary, multi-gender Hebrew online and share it as an image.

What is multi-gender Hebrew?

Hebrew marks gender almost everywhere: in verbs, adjectives, pronouns and plurals. Saying “you are invited” means choosing between a masculine and a feminine form, and the usual workarounds, such as slashes (לומד/ת) or listing both forms, are long and still leave out nonbinary people.

Multi-Gender Hebrew (עברית רב־מגדרית), designed by Michal Shomer, solves this with new letters. Each one stands in a single spot of a word for the difference between the masculine and the feminine form, so one word speaks to women, men and nonbinary people alike. For example, לומד׍ replaces “לומד/ת” (learning), and תלמיד׊׉ replaces “תלמידים ותלמידות” (male and female students).

The system has 12 characters: 11 letters and one vowel mark. They exist in the Alef MultiGndr font, and on a device without that font they show up as empty boxes. This tool gets around that: you write here, and the text becomes an image that looks the same on every device.

How to use the tool

  1. Type or paste Hebrew text into the box above.
  2. To add a multi-gender letter, put the cursor where you want it and click one of the 12 buttons under the box. The character goes in at the cursor.
  3. Click “Copy”. On a computer the image is copied to the clipboard, ready to paste (Ctrl+V) into a chat, an email or a document. On a phone, the device’s share sheet opens. Where copying or sharing is not available, the image downloads as a PNG file.

The image is 1200 pixels wide, and its height depends on the length of the text. The box has a maximum height, and once you reach it no more text can be added. The 🌓 button switches between a light and a dark background, and the image uses the colours you chose. A small signature with the tool’s version number appears in the corner of the image. Everything happens in your browser; the text is never sent to a server.

Plurals use two letters together: ׊׉ instead of ־ים / ־ות (תלמיד׊׉), and ׊׍ in the construct form (תלמיד׊׍ הכיתה). The rev mark goes after the multi-gender letter; according to the official site, Firefox and Safari support it fully, and in other browsers its position may be slightly off. In words that end in a final letter, such as הולך / הולכת, the recommendation is to write the regular letter: הולכ׍, מבינ׌.

Examples

  • ברוכ׊׉ הבא׊׉! — welcome (to everyone)
  • את׌ מוזמנ׍ לנסות. — you are invited to try
  • כל התלמיד׊׉ מוזמנ׊׉ לטיול. — all the students are invited on the trip
  • ה׊א אמר׌ שהספר של׎. — he/she said the book is his/hers
  • ה׊א ׏גיע מחר. — he/she will arrive tomorrow

Why an image instead of text

The new letters sit in unused code points of the Hebrew block of Unicode, so most devices cannot display them without the matching font. An image looks the same for everyone: in WhatsApp, Telegram, email, social media and documents. To type multi-gender Hebrew directly on your computer, install the font and the keyboard from the official site.
